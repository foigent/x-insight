## Necessary contract additions

1. **P1 — Prepared replacement also expires.** When XI's authoritative current digest still names the original expired, unrevoked token, but its one `prepared` replacement has also expired, a recovery ticket must bind that exact current digest and the observed prepared operation/digest. In the owner-controlled recovery transaction, lock installation, identity, authoritative current token, and exact pending operation; recheck original binding, unchanged scope, both administrators' approved flow, nonrevocation, and the replacement's actual expiry. Atomically make that one stale operation terminal, revoke its exact expired replacement, consume the ticket, and prepare the new replacement from the still-current original digest. A stale operation must remain terminal and never reopen; its expired bearer may be rejected before operation lookup. A live prepared replacement is still eligible for query/confirm and must not be discarded by this exception. This releases the one-open-operation constraint without a cleanup scheduler or a second recovery platform.

2. **P1 — XI committed, UR did not promote, and the committed replacement expires.** XI's authoritative current digest is now the committed replacement; the original token has been revoked. XI may issue a recovery ticket only for that current expired but unrevoked digest. UR must match it against the encrypted secret in its exact durable pending operation, not against its obsolete local current bearer. After fixed-origin XI ticket resolution proves that digest is authoritative, UR can transactionally reconcile the committed pending secret as its local current predecessor, retaining the operation/receipt history, then use the ordinary recovery prepare/query/confirm/CAS path. If the pending secret is absent or mismatched, stop; the revoked original secret cannot substitute. Reconciliation does not change `control_fence`, original installation/redeem ACK, scope, or history.

3. **P1 — Ticket and recovery CAS.** Ticket issuance and redemption must bind XI's current digest plus the exact pending-state snapshot (none or one expired prepared operation). Redemption must reject a changed current digest, live or different pending operation, revoked current token/identity, revoked installation or scope, and expired ticket. For the committed/UR-not-promoted case, XI has no open operation to abandon; the UR local reconciliation is the necessary extra pre-step. Recovery's later query/confirm and local promotion remain the shared path.

## Authentication expiry interleaving

`AuthenticateServiceToken` calls `queryServiceIdentityByDigest(..., false)`, which uses `FOR UPDATE OF i,t` in the same transaction before comparing identity expiry and executing `UPDATE service_identities SET status='expired'`. The existing work-event renewal also locks `i,t` before extension. Thus, for transactions that lock the same identity row, expiry marking and extension serialize: an authenticator waiting on a committed extension reads the new expiry; an extender waiting on an authenticator sees `expired` and must take the approved recovery path. The alleged stale-read/update interleaving is **not demonstrated by the present lock path**. The new connector rotate/recover implementation must acquire the identity row lock before changing its expiry/status. Adding `AND expires_at <= $2` to the expiry UPDATE is a small defensive predicate, not a substitute for that lock. A controlled PG interleaving regression should exercise auth-versus-extension and auth-versus-owner-recovery under the actual roles before declaring the lifecycle complete.

## Conclusion

Both expired-pending front states have a bounded same-installation recovery path. The shared contract needs these transitions before freeze; otherwise its one-open-operation rule can permanently block recovery, or UR can incorrectly present a revoked original as predecessor. Runtime and PG behavior remain unverified in this read-only follow-up.
